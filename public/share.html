Stream specs
Capture
Source resolution, capped at 1920×1080 (downscaled to fit, never upscaled). Up to 30 fps. Screen and window capture only produce a frame when the picture changes, so a still screen sends very few frames; tab capture runs at a steady rate.
To OBS
WebRTC, peer to peer (the server only sets the connection up). Codec negotiated by the browsers, up to 8 Mbps, 30 fps max. Bitrate adapts to the network and favours low latency (only a short jitter buffer on the receiving end), so a congested link shows as lower quality or dropped frames rather than growing delay.
Sound
Opus, 48 kHz stereo, up to 192 kbps, with echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain all off. Sent to the OBS display only.
Monitors
The control panel's Preview and Program monitors get a separate encode: one third size, 15 fps max, 600 kbps max, no sound.
Encoding load
This computer encodes one stream per viewer (OBS plus each open monitor), so a slow machine can drop frames. "Limited by: cpu" in the live stats means just that.
